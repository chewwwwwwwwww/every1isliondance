Behind the ritual

Old joy. Your movement.

福 (fú) means blessing or good fortune. Southern lion dance brings expressive movement, rhythmic percussion and community together. This is a playful contemporary interpretation, rather than a traditional dance lesson.

Your camera is processed locally. Highlights contain the active drawing rounds and celebration, with countdowns and resets omitted. You can also make a 3× timelapse with the percussion kept at its original tempo. Choosing Share by QR uploads your poster and optional video to a public link. The creator can remove that shared keepsake.

Each dancer receives a random matching headgear and outfit, inspired by Fut San and Hok San southern lions. Headgear sits above the forehead so your face stays visible. Close-up views show headgear; the outfit appears when your torso is clearly tracked. The sets stay yours throughout the dance.

Connected character sections are drawn together in short rounds. More complex parts receive longer drawing windows. Blessing phrases use one flowing interpretation per character, with 8–12 seconds based on its stroke count. Everyone in a simultaneous round gets the longest assigned window. Every gold mark on your red packet comes from a participant’s recorded brush path. We arrange and uniformly scale the strokes into the character; we keep their shapes. The reference glyph appears only as a movement guide.
